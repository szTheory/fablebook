## Common Pitfalls

### Treating compilation as proof of safety

**What goes wrong:** A compiler classification remains even though a parser front interns caller-derived names.  
**How to avoid:** Trace `file/1` through `file/2` to `filenames/3`, and trace both the input filename and action identifier provenance from `.xrl` source. Record explicit per-MFA verdict and replacement disposition.  
**Warning signs:** A reason that describes output type or load behavior but does not mention atom creation.

### Citing a source that the offline resolver cannot resolve

**What goes wrong:** A test or semantic record cites `compile.leex.ex` but the archive manifest has no exact core/floor record, so citation resolution cannot establish provenance.  
**How to avoid:** Add both exact lane source files and manifest rows, update the archive digest in the existing evidence helper, then cite the now-bound source. No recurring fetch.  
**Warning signs:** A source map row exists but the manifest row or archive bytes do not.

### Making the guard probe execute Leex

**What goes wrong:** A test may create caller-derived atoms while attempting to prove the guard.  
**How to avoid:** Keep the banned call in an uncalled function body; compile/inspect only.  
**Warning signs:** Invoking `:leex.file`, generating a scanner, or asserting runtime atom-count behavior.
